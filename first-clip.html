Three habits that make a reading list worth keeping

By Roan · Clipwise · 2 minute read

Most reading lists fail the same way. Saving is easy, so everything gets saved, and a month later the list is a few hundred links that all look equally important. Nothing is wrong with the tool. What's missing is a reason to come back.

The fix is not to save less, but to save the part that made you stop. These three habits help with that.

1. Mark the sentence, not the page

When something is worth saving, there is usually one line that made it so. Highlight that line before you save. Later, you read the line first and only open the whole page when you need it.

A saved page tells you what you read. A highlighted sentence tells you why it mattered.

2. Give every page a status

A simple status column does more than any tag system. Three values are enough:

  • To read: saved, not opened yet
  • Reading: started, worth finishing
  • Done: read, with the useful part highlighted

3. Look back once a week

Pick ten minutes on the same day each week. Skim the highlights of what you finished and move anything stale out of "To read". It is the only part that takes discipline, and it is what turns a pile of links into something you actually use.
